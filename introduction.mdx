The Engineering Growth framework is a structured, eight-month self-study plan designed for senior backend engineers who want to build durable systems expertise — not just familiarity with the current tool cycle. Its thesis is simple: **depth over breadth, mastery over trends.** Rather than skimming a dozen technologies, you go deep on the handful of systems that every large-scale backend ultimately reduces to — databases, caches, message brokers, and observability — while running a parallel AI track that keeps you fluent in the layer that is increasingly living on top of all of them. By the end, you will not just know the names; you will be able to explain mechanisms, trace design decisions back through history, and articulate the trade-offs that make any given choice right or wrong for a given workload.

---

## The Three Tracks

The plan runs three parallel tracks with deliberately unequal weight. Systems fundamentals get the majority of your hours because that is what makes everything else legible. AI work runs lighter but consistently throughout so it compounds. Interview prep is always-on from Month 2, not bolted on at the end.

## The 8-Month Arc

The Track A syllabus is sequential by design. Each topic is the prerequisite for the next. You do not move on until the depth bar for the current topic is met.

| Period | Track A Focus | Track B | Track C |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Month 1–2** | Databases — DDIA Ch 1–7, MySQL/InnoDB internals, Cassandra concepts | Paper reading, ~45 min/week | Begins Month 2 |
| **Month 3** | Caching — Redis internals, eviction, patterns, invalidation | Paper + one agent-framework file/week | 2 algo/week, 1 design writeup/2 weeks |
| **Month 4** | Messaging — Kafka partitioning, consumer groups, exactly-once, Raft | **Scale up.** Choose and start your project | Monthly mock interview begins |
| **Month 5** | Reliability + Observability — tracing, metrics, OTEL, SLOs | Project build | Active prep |
| **Month 6+** | Deep Choice — read source code for one system (InnoDB, Kafka, or etcd/TiKV) | Project deployed and observable | Job-search-ready checkpoint |
| **Month 7–8** | — | — | **Active interviewing** |

<Note>
Month 6+ is the plan's **elastic buffer**. If an earlier block runs long — for example, the Cassandra reading in Month 2 back-half — that time comes out of Month 6, not out of Months 3–5. Protect the core syllabus.
</Note>

## What You'll Build

Completing the plan produces four kinds of concrete output.

**Weekly artifacts.** One per week, every week. Artifacts are *transformed*, not transcribed — a synthesis, comparison, answer, or design the source material didn't contain. Adversarial exchanges, written design answers, and tier notes written from memory to the depth bar all count. A chapter summary does not.

**Five-line case study reductions.** Every case study terminates in this format:

```text
Problem:    what they were actually solving
Constraint: the requirement that forced the design
Trade:      what they gave up, explicitly
My miss:    what I'd have gotten wrong
Rule:       the transferable one-liner
```

"My miss" is the line that does the work. It is the only one you cannot write from skimming.

**A shippable Track B project.** By the end of Month 6, you will have deployed and instrumented one of: an agent eval and observability platform, a coding assistant with retrieval and memory, or an agent workflow orchestrator. This is the deliverable a hiring manager will actually ask about.

**Interview-ready state by Month 6.** The Month 6 checkpoint requires an unbroken weekly artifact cadence for the last several weeks, three to five artifacts strong enough to walk someone through, the Track B project deployed and observable, and recruiter conversations started.

## The Rules

The plan's rules are not administrative overhead. They are the mechanism that makes the plan work.

**Miss a day → drop it.** Do not make it up. The plan is not a backlog. Attempting to recover missed days turns the next day into a catch-up exercise and compounds across weeks.

**Miss a week → Sunday resets, doesn't catch up.** The Sunday review resets the clock. You do not reconstruct the lost week. The plan covers eight months; one dropped week does not break the outcome. Attempting to catch up does.

**One artifact per week.** Recorded in the Sunday review's Q1, which is the ledger. There is no separate tracking table.

**One long project, not five.** Track B is one shippable project that you build over several months. Pivoting mid-track restarts the compounding.

These constraints feel strict because they are. The purpose is to prevent the most common failure mode in long self-study plans: accumulated debt that turns the plan into a psychological weight instead of an engine. Treating a missed day as gone — not as debt — keeps the plan lightweight enough to sustain.

<Note>
**Mentor 1:1 cadence.** A standing monthly 1:1 with your mentor is part of the plan's structure. The format is simple: walk them through the month's artifact. Not a status report — a technical explanation. If you cannot walk them through it, the artifact is not done. Schedule these at the start of each month so they do not get dropped when the month gets busy.
</Note>
